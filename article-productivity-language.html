Productivity, Politics, and the Abuse of Language

Editorial Board · Renewal News Network

It has become increasingly common for public officials to declare meetings, days, or even entire administrations “productive.” The term is deployed confidently, but rarely defined.

Productivity, properly understood, is a measurable relationship between effort and outcome. In politics, however, the word is often used as a rhetorical substitute for accountability.

When leaders announce productivity without reference to concrete results, timelines, or public benefit, language ceases to clarify and begins to obscure. Managerial vocabulary is used to simulate effectiveness rather than demonstrate it.

Democratic accountability requires more than activity. It requires outcomes that can be examined, contested, and judged. Without this, “productivity” becomes a performance rather than a standard.

— Editorial Board
