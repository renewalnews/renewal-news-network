Titles, Authority, and the Culture of Unearned Deference

Editorial Board · Renewal News Network

Public life increasingly confuses titles with authority. Honorifics are treated as substitutes for argument, and formal labels are allowed to stand in for credibility.

This confusion weakens democratic discourse. Authority arises from evidence, reasoning, and accountability — not from the casual inflation of status markers.

When societies reward appearance over substance, they encourage deference without justification and discourage scrutiny where it is most needed.

Democratic culture depends on resisting this temptation. Respect must be earned through clarity, integrity, and public responsibility — not conferred by title alone.

— Editorial Board
